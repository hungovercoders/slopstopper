🔏 The small print

Privacy

What this site collects, what its host keeps, and which other services its pages reach. A tool whose checks audit other sites' headers and third-party loads should be able to say exactly what its own site does, so this page is kept in step with the site's configuration.

What this site collects

Nothing of its own.

There are no accounts, no forms, no cookies, and no analytics or tracking scripts.

The pages are static. The only scripts they load are the site's own: copy.js, which powers the copy buttons on code blocks, and feedback.js, which loads the comments widget on the feedback page when you ask for it. The Content-Security-Policy on every path allows scripts and styles from this origin alone and none inline, so an analytics snippet or beacon cannot run even if one were injected. One page relaxes that policy for the comments widget, described below.

Hosting

Cloudflare serves the pages.

Like any host, Cloudflare processes your IP address and browser details to deliver each page. It keeps aggregate traffic statistics (how many requests, from which countries, with which status codes). Per-request logging (Cloudflare's Workers Logs) is switched off for this site, so no record of your individual visit is kept for the maintainers to read. The Worker that serves the site only redirects and serves files; it logs nothing of its own. Cloudflare's handling of that data is described in its privacy policy. Nothing from it is combined with anything else or used to identify you.

Other services the pages reach

One, on one page.

  • Giscus, on the feedback page only, and only when you ask. The feedback page offers comments from Giscus. Nothing is fetched from giscus.app until you press "Load comments"; pressing it loads a script from Giscus in an iframe under a page-scoped exception to the site's Content-Security-Policy, and sends your IP address, browser details and the page address to Giscus. Reading the page without pressing it sends nothing to anyone but Cloudflare. Commenting means signing in to GitHub, so GitHub and Giscus both see that visit and your GitHub identity, and the comment itself is stored as a GitHub Discussion on the SlopStopper repository. See Giscus's privacy policy and GitHub's privacy statement. The exception, what leaves the site through it, and how the loader is pinned are written up in the CSP exceptions doc.
  • Fonts are served from this site, not from a font service.
  • Links go to GitHub and hungovercoders.com. Following one is a visit to that site, under its own policy.
The CLI and the workflows

They never talk to this site.

The slopstopper-cli package and the ss-* workflows run in your own repository and your own CI. They send nothing to slopstopper.dev. The tools they drive fetch their own inputs from their own sources (Semgrep its rules, Playwright its browsers, Trivy its vulnerability database), and the Lighthouse report stays in your repo unless you opt in to its public upload.
