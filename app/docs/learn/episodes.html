The YouTube title, thumbnail and description conventions for the "From zero to slopstopper" series, and the recording notes every episode follows, so the series reads as one thing. Each lesson's own script is the collapsed "Episode script" at the end of its page; this file is what those scripts have in common.

Title

From zero to slopstopper #N: <lesson title> for the numbered lessons, and From zero to slopstopper: <topic> for an occasional extra. Keep it under 60 characters so it survives the mobile crop; a lesson title with a colon in it drops the part after the colon.

Thumbnail

Stoppy, the site's mascot (app/og-image.svg carries the drawing), the episode number large, three to five words of the lesson title, and one frame from the episode's terminal. The site's brand tokens, which demos/theme.json holds as a terminal theme: the cream background, the ink text and the orange accent, so a row of thumbnails looks like one series.

Description

Fill in every <…>. Keep the first line under 120 characters; it is the line search results show.

<One sentence: what you can do after this episode.>

This is lesson <N> of From zero to slopstopper, a course on the quality
gates that keep AI-assisted repos honest: one check per lesson, the slop
it stops, what it looks like when it fires, and the fix. Every lesson
runs in a checkout of the slopstopper repo, so you can follow along with
git, mise and Python.

Recording notes

  • Every episode opens on its title card: the series label, the lesson's title and one command, then the lesson's cold open. The row of episodes reads as one series that way, and a viewer who lands mid-course knows where they are before the first terminal appears.
  • One terminal, 100 columns by 30 rows, font size 16, the site's theme from demos/theme.json, and nothing else on screen but the occasional zoom the lesson's shot list names. The lesson's tape, docs/learn/tapes/NN.tape, reproduces the terminal parts on demand at exactly that size, so b-roll can be re-recorded without re-doing the voice.
  • Say the command before typing it, then pause on the output for a beat longer than feels natural. Viewers read slower than you talk, and a red line needs a moment to land.
  • Show the report, not just the verdict. Every check's red line names a report; the episode opens it, because that is what the viewer will do next week on their own repo.
  • Every episode ends on the checkpoint passing, then the one-line pitch for the next lesson.

The tapes

task contributing:learn:tapes writes every lesson's tape from its runnable blocks, so a tape cannot drift from the lesson; CI fails when one is stale. task contributing:learn:record -- NN records a tape into dist/learn/NN.mp4, the b-roll for the edit. What each tape needs and how it differs from the README's demo tapes is in docs/contributing/DEMOS.md.

The rest of an episode, the cards, the captions, the filled description and the thumbnail, is cut by hand for now. apic's course has a generator that builds all of that from a shot list; whether a port pays for itself here is decided after the first two episodes are recorded by hand.
